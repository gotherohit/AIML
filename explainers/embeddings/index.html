Embeddings: the AI that finds instead of writes (EmbeddingGemma 2)

EmbeddingGemma 2 can't write a word: it turns sentences, photos and clips into lists of numbers that point the same way when they mean the same thing. This short article explains the idea, with a real run.

In one line: an embedding model doesn't write; it turns anything (a sentence, a photo, a clip) into a list of numbers, placed so that similar meanings point the same way. That makes it a search engine's core, not a chatbot.

A model that can't write a word

On 6 October 2026 Google DeepMind released EmbeddingGemma 2, open weights under Apache 2.0. Ask it for a poem and it replies with a list of numbers. That's not a bug: it's an embedding model, and those numbers are more useful than they look.

What an embedding is

An embedding model takes something, like a sentence, and turns it into a list of 768 numbers. Think of that list as a location: an arrow pointing to a spot on a map of meaning. A sentence with a similar meaning points almost the same way.

How close are two arrows? Multiply the matching numbers of the two lists (1st with 1st, 2nd with 2nd, all 768) and add everything up: the dot product. This model scales every list it outputs to length 1, and for length-1 lists the dot product is the cosine of the angle between the arrows. Smaller angle, higher score. From my own run:

PairScoreAngle
"Happy dog on the grass" vs a sentence about a puppy fetching sticks0.95about 18°
"Happy dog on the grass" vs a sentence about tax returns0.82about 35°
Read the ranking, not the number. All scores from this model are fairly high: even unrelated things scored 0.67 to 0.84 in my test. 0.82 does not mean "82% similar". What matters is which item scores highest, and by how much.

So this is not a writer like Gemini or GPT: text in, new text out. It is closer to a "finder" (my nickname, not an official term). Google calls it an embedding model, used for semantic search and retrieval; inside a chatbot that looks things up (RAG), it is the retriever.

I ran it: one map for text, photos and video

I gave it sentences, photos and short video clips about dogs, food, cars and the sea, on a laptop CPU with no API. Each one became 768 numbers. Squashed down to 3 numbers so they can be plotted (a PCA projection), the items gathered by topic: dogs in one region, food, cars and the sea in others, with no labels given.

  • The closest sentence-and-photo pair was a pasta sentence and a pasta photo: 0.89.
  • For the dog sentence, the 2 dog photos scored highest (0.87 and 0.85) and the 2 video clips lowest (0.76 and 0.75). The gaps are small.
  • Searching for "waves on a beach", the 2 beach photos came out on top (0.73 and 0.72, then 0.63).
How to read the map. A 3D projection of 768 numbers loses detail, so the scores are the real measurement, not the picture. Text, photos and clips also sit in their own regions (a sentence is always closer to other sentences than to any photo), so for the map each group was shifted to a common centre to show topic rather than file type. It's a small illustration with hand-picked samples, not a benchmark.

What's new in version 2

Version 1 only understood text. Version 2 takes text, code, images, video and audio and puts them all in one shared space, so a sentence can be compared with a photo. It is built from 3 parts:

PartParameters
Text model270 million
Vision encoder170 million
Audio encoder300 million
Total740 million

You can load only the parts you need. Its 8K context fits up to 29 images, 58 video frames or about 5.5 minutes of audio in one input.

It fits on a phone

Google's figures, quantized, on a Pixel 11 Pro: about 567 MB of memory for the full model, about 191 MB for text only. You can also keep fewer numbers: cut each list from 768 to 512, 256 or 128 (so-called Matryoshka embeddings), for up to 6× less storage. The catch, per the model card: quality barely changes at 256, but at 128 images and video get noticeably worse. (A list cut to 128 is also no longer length 1, so re-normalise it before comparing scores.)

What it's for

Search: your own photos and videos, or finding the right document for a chatbot to read. It's on Hugging Face and Kaggle and works with Ollama.
